Consistent with reports that mating success in medaka is determined more by courtship rate than by aggressive dominance (Kondo et al. 2023; Weir & Grant 2010). No stable correlation was found between aggression frequency and paternity, aggression count was zero in most trials(B); father male were distinguished by courtship frequence, and wrap attempt frequence(A.C). The male leading in courtship before the spawning event was more likely to become the father(12 leader 2 tie and 1 lower in 15 spawning).
Wrapping success rate of courtship is 37.5%(A). Females selectively accepted or rejected wrapping attempts. Median duration of accepted wrappings was 27 s versus 3 s for rejected ones(B). Success rates differed across three groups defined by courtship leadership at the spawning boundary (leading: 44.8%, trailing: 14.3%, tied: 60.0%)(C).
Female spawning motivation may be dynamically elevated by male behaviour immediately preceding spawning. Females select a mating partner might based on: earlier courtship initiation(A), higher courtship frequency(C), shorter courtship inter-bout intervals(E), and higher wrapping frequency(D).
After spawning, the quick circle almost disappears, and following continues as a guarding behavior（Kondo & Awata, Sci Rep 2025）. The significant difference in courtship frequency between sires and non-sires disappeared post-spawning. However, unlike the sharp drop reported in prior work, a substantial proportion of males maintained high courtship frequency even after spawning.
Successful spawning might required a sufficient acclimation period: pairs established for approximately 18–21 days or more showed stable spawning. 35mins recording windows substantially underestimate spawning rates (56% within window vs. 77% including same-day post-window spawning).
